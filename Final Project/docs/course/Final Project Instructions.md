# CEOR Final Project Instructions

## Overview

The final project is designed to highlight key concepts in **applied health services research**.  
Each team is assigned a dataset and is expected to complete an empirical analysis based on that dataset.

The project has **two graded components**:

| Component | Description | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oral Presentation | Group presentation delivered before the paper submission | 20% |
| Term Paper | Final manuscript-style paper | 20% |
| **Total** | Combined final project grade | **40%** |

## Deliverables

### 1. Oral Presentation

- Scheduled for **Session 13**
- Should follow the same general structure as the term paper:
  - Introduction
  - Methods
  - Results
  - Discussion
- Expected length: **approximately 15 minutes**
- All team members should present at least one component of the study
- Instructor feedback from the presentation should be incorporated before the final paper submission

### 2. Term Paper

- Due in **Session 14**
- Should be written in the style of a **peer-reviewed manuscript**
- Required sections:
  - Introduction
  - Materials and Methods
  - Results
  - Discussion

## Manuscript Expectations

The paper should:

- explain the clinical or epidemiologic question under study
- describe the dataset and analytic approach clearly
- present results in both narrative and tabular/figure form
- interpret the findings in the context of the existing literature
- discuss limitations and future directions

At minimum, include:

- **Table 1**: cohort characteristics
- **A second results table**: modeling results

Subset analyses may also be described if relevant.

## Formatting Requirements

- 1-inch margins
- 12-point font
- Double-spaced
- Maximum length: **2,000 words**
- References required: **at least 10, no more than 20**

## Writing Style

### General guidance

- Prefer active voice whenever possible
- Avoid overly long dependent clauses
- Use clear action verbs
- Prioritize readability and precision

### Practical principle

Write as if you are explaining the study to a well-trained student. Even knowledgeable reviewers value clarity.

## Section-by-Section Guidance

### I. Introduction

### Required content

- State the research question
- Provide a concise background
- Define the research aims, hypotheses, and outcomes

### Recommended structure

Use **three paragraphs** for the entire Introduction.

#### Paragraph 1

- 4-6 sentences
- Define the broader clinical or epidemiologic problem
- Explain why the problem matters

#### Paragraph 2

- 5-6 sentences
- Identify the knowledge gap
- Summarize prior literature briefly
- Explain why the gap is important

#### Paragraph 3

- State the study purpose directly
- Link the study objective to the gap identified above
- Present the study hypothesis or aims clearly

### II. Materials and Methods

### Required content

#### Study population

- Describe the study sample
- State that the data come from **NIS**
- Summarize the data collection process and study population
- Reference:
  - <http://www.hcup-s.ahrq.gov/db/nation/nis/nisdbdocumentation.jsp>

#### Methods

- Describe the statistical methods used
- Explain assumptions associated with those methods
- Explain how assumptions were assessed
- Describe how covariates were chosen
- State the alpha level
- State the software used

### What reviewers should understand after reading Methods

- the study design
- the data source
- inclusion and exclusion criteria
- variables collected
- how the data were reviewed
- whether the validity of the data source or variables is supported by prior research

### Writing guidance

- Keep the section organized and easy to follow
- Avoid copying text from IRB materials
- Do not overwhelm the reader with unnecessary detail
- The goal is to show that the study was conducted thoughtfully and appropriately

### III. Results

### Required content

- Describe the cohort overall
- Show the distribution of exposure/treatment and outcomes
- Include **Table 1** for patient and hospital characteristics
- Include any additional relevant tables or figures
- Report logistic regression, log-linear Poisson regression, or propensity score analysis results, as applicable

### Statistical reporting expectations

Report:

- final covariates in the model
- odds ratios or risk ratios
- confidence intervals
- p-values
- test statistics when appropriate

Do **not** report p-values alone.

### Suggested structure

#### Paragraph 1

- Present the analytic cohort
- Include a flow diagram if applicable
- Reference Figure 1 and Table 1 briefly

#### Paragraph 2

- Present the primary outcome
- Summarize the main statistical findings
- Consider presenting the primary results in Table 2

#### Paragraph 3

- Present secondary outcomes or other notable findings

### Writing guidance

- Use tables and figures to carry detailed numeric results
- Avoid listing statistics exhaustively in the text
- Aim for no more than **five to six paragraphs**

### IV. Discussion

### Required content

- Interpret the primary findings
- State whether the hypothesis was supported
- Explain possible reasons for the findings
- Describe practical implications
- Discuss limitations
- Suggest future research directions

### Suggested structure

#### Paragraph 1

- Focus on the primary outcome
- State the most important finding clearly

#### Paragraph 2

- Address secondary outcomes

#### Paragraphs 3-4

- Interpret the findings in clinical, research, and epidemiologic context
- Relate the study to the existing literature
- Suggest future directions

#### Paragraph 5

- Discuss strengths, limitations, and future work

### Writing guidance

- Keep the Discussion focused and concise in the first draft
- Expect collaborators and reviewers to request additions later

### V. Contribution Statement

Include a brief description of each team member’s contribution to the final project.

## Notes

- An abstract is **not required**
- References **are required**
- Maximum references: **20**

> Dessources K, Hou JY, Tergas AI, Burke WM, Ananth CV, Prendergast E, Chen L, Neugut AI, Hershman DL, Wright JD. Factors associated with 30-day hospital readmission after hysterectomy. *Obstetrics and Gynecology*. 2015 Feb;125(2):461-70.
